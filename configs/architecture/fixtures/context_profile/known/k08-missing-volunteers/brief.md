# Harbour Food Bank: a volunteer rota app

Harbour Food Bank is a charity in Portwell. We collect donated food and deliver parcels to people who cannot get to us, mostly older and housebound clients. Volunteers do the driving, the packing and the phone calls. Today we coordinate them with a shared spreadsheet, a WhatsApp group and a lot of phone calls from our coordinator, Maureen.

We want a small app where volunteers can see open shifts, sign up for them and see the deliveries they have accepted. Coordinators would post shifts and match deliveries to drivers. Nothing has been built yet; this note is the start.

## Who would use it

Honestly, we don't know how many volunteers we have. It is somewhere between a few dozen and a few hundred, depending on who you count, and nobody has gone through the spreadsheet. We also don't know how many deliveries we do a week, because the drivers' paper slips are never added up. December is our busiest month, but nobody has measured by how much.

## Money

We have not set a budget for this. The trustees will decide at their January meeting whether to spend anything at all, and how much. We also don't know what running it each month could cost, or who would pay for that.

## People

Our IT is one volunteer, Dev, a retired network engineer, who gives us about five hours a week. He knows Linux and networks; he has not built a web app before. We already use Google Workspace for Nonprofits for email and documents, and the trustees have ruled that any tool coordinators use must sign them in with their Google Workspace accounts, not new passwords.

## Data and privacy

The app would hold volunteers' names, phone numbers and availability, and clients' names, addresses and delivery notes such as "leave with the neighbour" or "use the side door, client is in a wheelchair". Our data protection policy says a client's address may only be shown to the volunteer who has accepted that delivery, and must disappear from the volunteer's view once the delivery is done. We are registered with the Information Commissioner's Office.

## Reliability

If the app is down for a day, Maureen goes back to the phone and the spreadsheet; that is annoying, but nobody goes hungry. Losing the record of who accepted which delivery on the day would be worse, because parcels could go undelivered.

## What we want

Above all, something in volunteers' hands before the winter appeal starts in November, if we can manage it. After that, it should cost as little as possible to run. And Dev should not have to babysit it.
