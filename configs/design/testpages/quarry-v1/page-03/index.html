How a pickup works

One caddy, one morning a week. You never carry scraps further than your front step.

  1. Step 1

    Fill the caddy

    The 7-litre caddy lives under your sink with a roll of compostable liners. Peels, grounds and plate scrapings go in as you cook.

  2. Step 2

    Put the bin out on Tuesday

    Tip the caddy into the lockable outdoor bin and leave it on your step or by your building door before 7 a.m.

  3. Step 3

    We swap it

    Our driver leaves a freshly washed bin and takes the full one to the yard. You get a text when it's done.

What your scraps become

A household fills about 3.5 kg of scraps a week. Over a year that comes to about 180 kg, which the Mill Lane yard turns into about 60 kg of finished compost.

Each April you can collect a 10-litre bag for your pots and window boxes. Whatever you leave goes to the community gardens next to the yard.

Questions

What people ask before their first round.

Does the caddy smell?
The lid seals and has a charcoal filter. When the caddy is full, tip it into the larger bin we leave outside; most homes do that twice a week.
What can go in?
Fruit and vegetable peels, coffee grounds, tea bags, eggshells, bread and cooked leftovers. Not bones, plastic or cat litter.
I live in a flat. Does that work?
Yes. Leave the bin by your building's main door or with the concierge, and tell us where when you book.
What if I miss a Tuesday?
Keep the bin until the following week and we take both loads then. Going away? Pause before Monday at 6 p.m.
Who runs Loamwise?
Ana Ruiz and Theo Park, who also run the Mill Lane compost yard. Write to them at hello@loamwise.example.
Can I stop at any time?
Yes. Cancel whenever you like; we collect the bin on your last round.
