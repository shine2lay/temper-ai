About Roomwise

Roomwise began in a shared office where four teams fought over two meeting rooms. Paper sheets on the doors were always out of date, and the calendar said a room was taken when nobody was in it.

We built a board that shows what is really going on: who has a room, until when, and whether they turned up. If nobody checks in, the room goes back on the board for someone else.

Today the board runs offices of every size, from a ten-person studio to a bank with three hundred rooms, and it still does that one job before anything else.

We are a small team of eleven people spread over three cities. We meet in person every quarter, in whichever office has a free room, and the board tells us which one that is.

Roomwise is paid for by the offices that use it. We have no advertisers and no investors to answer to, so the plan is simple: keep the board fast, honest and easy to read.
