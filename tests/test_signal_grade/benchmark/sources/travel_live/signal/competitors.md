### Read

**Crowded-and-growing.** Three well-reviewed, actively-funded dedicated AI trip planners (Mindtrip, Layla, Wanderlog) plus every major OTA (Expedia, Booking.com, Trip.com) and OpenAI itself building the same chat-to-itinerary feature. The Expedia acquisition of Layla (Jul 2026) is a strong real-money signal that incumbents see this as valuable, not hypothetical. The wedge (preferences → personal day-by-day itinerary with bookable links) is already being built by funded competitors with tens of thousands of real reviews — this is a real, proven demand pattern, but differentiation against free OTA-bundled AI assistants (Romie, Booking.com's) will be hard since they're zero-marginal-cost add-ons to platforms with existing booking funnels.

**Confidence: high** — multiple independently-corroborated review counts, funding figures from press (TechCrunch, PhocusWire), and a real acquisition event.

**Confidence: low** — absence of incumbents could mean thin demand or a genuine gap; could not verify traffic to any of the small quiz sites (no SimilarWeb-style figures were quoted anywhere found), so this read rests on the absence of evidence rather than positive evidence of either case.

### Read

**Thin-or-none on the specific wedge, but the adjacent data-layer is crowded and proven.** Three products (Polarsteps, TripIt, Wanderlog) already hold large, well-reviewed user bases that capture exactly the past-trip data V3 wants to mine (locations visited, bookings, photos/maps) — so the *data substrate* and the *consumer habit* of logging trips is validated at real scale (Polarsteps alone claims 10M travelers, TripIt has 307K App Store ratings). But none of them currently do destination/stay/activity *recommendation* from that history — it's pure storage/organization today. This is the clearest "untapped, not absent" case in the four candidates: the audience and their data already exist inside incumbent apps, but the recommendation layer on top is missing. Also notable: TripIt shows a stark divergence between App Store rating (4.8/5, 307K) and Trustpilot rating (1.7/5, 47) — likely reflects that Trustpilot skews toward people who had a bad support experience and went looking for a complaints forum, a useful reminder to weight review-platform selection bias when reading "vitals" elsewhere in this report too.

**Confidence: med** — strong evidence on the data-layer incumbents, but genuinely no visibility into whether anyone has tried and failed at the recommendation layer specifically (survivorship — a failed attempt wouldn't necessarily leave review-platform traces).

### Read

**Crowded-but-weak-on-the-specific-wedge.** The advisor-software category (Travefy, Tern, and others like Nezasa/Travel Odyssey/Layup Travel mentioned as alternatives in search results) is real, paid, and reviewed — advisors already pay $31-59/mo (Travefy) or $25-49/seat/mo (Tern) for itinerary/CRM tooling, and Fora's $60M Series B/C plus 200 staff shows serious institutional money backing the broader "support independent advisors" thesis. But the specific wedge — an AI client-intake quiz that auto-drafts a matched proposal — was not found built by name in any of the three; existing complaints center on *missing costing/quote automation* (Travefy) and *compliance/trust gaps* (Fora), which is adjacent to, not the same as, "the AI doesn't understand my client's taste." That gap between proven willingness-to-pay for advisor tooling and the absence of an AI-matching feature specifically is the opening worth testing directly with advisors rather than inferring from these incumbents' review text alone.

**Confidence: med** — solid, multi-sourced data on the three named incumbents' vitals; lower confidence specifically on whether the AI-matching/proposal-drafting feature already exists inside one of them under a name not surfaced by these searches (e.g., as a recent product update), since advisor-software press coverage is sparser than consumer travel-app coverage.

## Cross-cutting notes

- **Vendor/listicle fabrication watch:** TripGenie's "+200% traffic" (Trip.com, self-reported) and Polarsteps' "10 million travelers" (self-reported) are both company claims with no independent corroboration found — both are flagged **estimate + vendor** above and should never be treated as the sole evidence of scale.
- **Review-platform divergence:** TripIt's App Store (4.8/5, 307K) vs. Trustpilot (1.7/5, 47) is a reminder that "rating" alone is meaningless without noting which platform and population it's drawn from — Trustpilot skews toward people actively seeking a complaints outlet.
